# Low vision and cognitive access

W3C, [Accessibility Requirements for People with Low Vision](https://www.w3.org/TR/low-vision-needs/). A working draft, not a conformance standard.

Sections 2.4 and 3.1 describe different brightness and contrast needs. Some people prefer light text on dark backgrounds. Others need light backgrounds or different colours. Sections 3.2–3.7 cover text size, spacing, reflow, nearby controls and keeping your place.

Shared goals for low vision and cognitive access:

- Offer light and dark themes, strong contrast and adjustable text.
- Keep labels, focus and reading position clear and stable.
- Offer reduced motion, a steady caret, screen-reader access and Read Aloud.

Check [text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html), [keyboard focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html) and [larger text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html) without losing controls. Test real desktops with users. Shared benefits are a design inference. No palette suits everyone.
